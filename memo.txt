\subsection{Decision note}

\begin{itemize}
    \item We keep the elapse day for the delay calculation instead of taking the difference between reporting and occurence week. This allows us to capture well the delay. In the case of week difference, if the event date is at the end of the week and the report happens at the beginning of the next week, then this has only 1 day of difference but if we take the week difference it will wrongly assign it a delay of 1 week. This will underestimate the delay d=0. 
    \item However when we aggregate the number of events by week, we use the Tuesday boundary. This is how we can maximize the number of events with d=0. 
    \item However I see that these definitions are inconsistent somehow. Because the age and delay of one event can be different. Eg: D=0 (if elapse day<7) but age = 1 (Difference between occurence week and report week).
    \item I will then keep the delay as difference of week.
    \item I will implement the reverse hazard model and compare with the forward that I currently have. 
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Definition and target}

Let $Y_t$ be the count for a fixed reporting horizon $B$ and $C_t(T)$ its observed subset at cutoff $T$. We make the following assumptions for our model:

\[Y_t \sim \operatorname{P}(\lambda_t)\] 

\begin{equation}
    log(\lambda_t) = f(t) \text{ where } f(.) \text{ is a smooth function}.
    \label{eq:2}
\end{equation}


If we define by $\rho_{t} = F_t(T-t)$ where $d$ is the reporting delay, then 
\[C_t(T) \mid Y_t \sim \operatorname{B}(Y_t, \rho_{t})\].

It then follows that 
\[\mathbb{E}[C_t(T)] = \mathbb{E}[Y_t] \cdot \rho_{t} \]

From \autoref{eq:2}, we get that
\begin{equation}
    log(\mathbb{E}[C_t(T)]) = log(\mu_t) = f(t) + log(\rho_{t}) .
    \label{eq:3}
\end{equation}

From this formulation we distinguish 3 different models: \autoref{eq:2}, \autoref{eq:3}, and

\begin{equation}
    log(\mathbb{E}[C_t(T)]) = log(\mu_t) = f(t)
    \label{eq:4}
\end{equation}

Our goal is to understand the difference between the three.
